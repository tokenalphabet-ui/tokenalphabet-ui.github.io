Altcoin

An altcoin is any cryptocurrency that is not Bitcoin. The word is short for "alternative coin" and caught on in 2011, when the first projects built on a copy of Bitcoin's code appeared, Namecoin in April and Litecoin in October.

Today the label covers thousands of very different things. Ethereum and Solana run smart contracts and apps, XRP was built for payments, USDT and USDC track the dollar, and Dogecoin started in 2013 as a joke. Some people leave stablecoins out and use the word only for coins with a floating price.

Taken together, altcoins are the part of the market that Bitcoin dominance leaves over. If Bitcoin holds 60% of the total market cap, every other coin, Ethereum included, shares the remaining 40%. When that share keeps growing for weeks, traders start calling it an altseason.

Altcoin prices usually swing harder than Bitcoin's, up and down. Many have small market caps and thin trading, and newer tokens often have a large part of their supply still locked for teams and early investors. Circulating supply, 24h volume and the age of a project say more about a coin than its price does.
