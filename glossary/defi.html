DeFi (decentralized finance)

DeFi, short for decentralized finance, is the name for financial services that run as smart contracts on a public blockchain. There is no company holding your money in between: you connect a self-custody wallet, sign a transaction and the contract settles the trade or the loan on-chain.

Most of it started on Ethereum. MakerDAO launched the DAI stablecoin in 2017, Uniswap went live in November 2018 and let people swap tokens against pools of liquidity instead of an order book, and lending markets such as Compound and Aave followed. Similar apps now run on Solana, BNB Chain, Arbitrum, Base and many other networks.

Because a protocol does not know who you are, loans are overcollateralized. To borrow $1,000 in stablecoins you might lock $1,500 worth of ETH. If ETH falls and the collateral drops below the protocol's threshold, a liquidator repays part of the debt and takes some of your ETH at a discount.

The size of the sector is usually quoted as total value locked (TVL), the dollar value of assets deposited in DeFi contracts. The risks are different from a bank's. A bug in a contract, a hacked bridge or a wrong price feed can cost users money, and nobody can reverse a transaction once it is confirmed.
